# 2026-07-10 Handover

This is a practical handover for the current state of the `nearly-g2-ode`
repository after the Berger, S^7, Podesta, Aloff-Wallach, Stiefel, and
`Sp(1) x Sp(1) x U(1)` exploration threads.

The short version is:

- The repository is a numerical laboratory for cohomogeneity-one nearly
  parallel G2 ODEs, especially singular-end Taylor germs and two-sided matching.
- The Berger and original S^7 `q_i` searches recovered the known homogeneous
  examples but found no new candidates.
- The Foscolo-Haskins S^6 detour showed that maximal-volume matching is often
  a better numerical loss than terminal singular shooting.
- The Podesta `SU(2)^3` action on S^7 was implemented and searched; it found
  only round and squashed compact closures.  A possible large-|a| uniqueness
  proof route made progress but ultimately stalled on a correlation/shaped-tube
  issue.
- Stiefel does not fit the current cohomogeneity-one endpoint pipeline in the
  obvious way.
- Aloff-Wallach `N_{1,1}` remains interesting, but it needs its own invariant
  form system and endpoint charts.
- The current active S^7 direction is the intermediate
  `Sp(1) x Sp(1) x U(1)` action on the `S3 x S3` orbit foliation.  The 13
  variable system and endpoint scout now exist.  A broad random max-volume
  scout recovered only the round/squashed controls and found no new candidate.

## Overall Purpose

The project tries to find, or convincingly fail to find, compact
cohomogeneity-one nearly parallel G2 structures beyond the known homogeneous
examples.

The initial source of truth is the eight-function `q_i` system for the
`SO(4)/Z_2^2` principal orbit.  The raw `q` equations are singular at the
endpoints, so the solver uses endpoint-adapted weighted variables `y_i`, builds
formal Taylor germs, and marches inward by Taylor re-expansion.  Candidate
metrics are tested either by:

- matching left and right raw `q` states at a prescribed midpoint, or
- marching each side to a maximal-volume principal orbit and matching there.

As the project expanded, the repository also gained separate invariant-form
systems for other cohomogeneity-one actions, especially Podesta's `SU(2)^3`
action and the newer `Sp(1) x Sp(1) x U(1)` intermediate action on S^7.

The important code layers are:

- `problem/`: the original Berger/S^7 `q_i` equation, endpoint data, weighted
  charts, and Taylor seeds.
- `solver/`: Taylor marching, two-sided matching, mirror shooting, and
  max-volume matching for the `q_i` system.
- `experiments/berger_space/`: Berger validation and search entrypoints.
- `experiments/s7/`: original `q_i`-based S^7 validation/search entrypoints.
- `experiments/s7/su2_cubed_*`: Podesta `SU(2)^3` action and large-|a| defect
  proof diagnostics.
- `experiments/s7/sp1xsp1xu1_*`: the new intermediate S^7 action.
- `experiments/aloff_wallach/`: first `N_{1,1}` ansatz, endpoint, and scouting
  plumbing.

## Berger Space

The Berger space was the main initial target.  The known homogeneous Berger
solution was recovered with the `q_i` system, and then a large sequence of
mirrored and non-mirrored searches was run.

The final curated audit is:

```text
docs/berger-branch-audit.md
```

Its conclusion is that no non-Berger nearly G2 candidate survived refinement or
high-order verification in the explored branches.

The main explored regimes were:

- mirrored Berger-near searches;
- non-mirrored 7D searches;
- vertex and cell-centered grids;
- symmetric physical `alpha/omega` intervals;
- positive `a,c`;
- negative `a,c`;
- mixed endpoint-local `mu` and square-root sign branches;
- positive-`a,c` and mixed-`mu` boundary follow-ups;
- max-volume Berger matching scouts.

The Berger-near and symmetric `alpha/omega` grids flow back to Berger under
refinement.  The positive-`a,c` and mixed-`mu` boundary regions produce
short-interval collapsed tails with nonzero residuals.  The remaining standard
branches do not show a low scout signal.

Representative audit table entry:

```text
near vertex:
  103680 seeds
  53284 branch-valid
  best scout residual about 2.5e-2
  81/89 selected refinements recovered Berger
  best final residual about 2.8e-12
```

No explored Berger branch produced a verified non-Berger candidate.

## Berger Collaborator Claim And Doubling Check

A collaborator reported a one-sided Berger-topology calculation with

```text
lambda = 6/sqrt(5)
mu = -1
nu = 0
a approximately 0.122983284928698
c approximately -0.074848833608095
```

Here `nu` in the collaborator's notation is `y3'(0)`, while this repository's
left parameter is `alpha = y2'(0) = -nu`.  Thus `nu=0` means `alpha=0`.

We reproduced the main one-sided scalar observation: after a tiny adjustment,

```text
a = 0.122983286360754
c = -0.074848833608095
nu = 0
```

the logarithmic volume derivative `l(pi/6)=V'/V` can be made about `1e-14` in
a direct raw integration check.  So the one-sided maximal-volume condition is
real.

However, it is not a smooth doubled solution.  The full mirror matching
residual at the collaborator point is about `1e-1`, not small.  Solving from
the left with these parameters also does not approach the right endpoint: the
real branch hits a degeneration around

```text
t approximately 0.778686
pi/3 - t approximately 0.2685
```

well before the right endpoint `pi/3`.

We also checked the doubling formula shown in the screenshot from the PDF.  If
the signs are taken literally, the induced map is

```text
(q1,q2,q3,q4,q5,q6,q7,q8)
 -> (q8,q4,-q6,-q2,-q7,-q3,q5,q1).
```

The full fixed-point equations force zero principal-orbit volume, so the
literal screenshot doubling cannot define a regular middle match.  A weaker
four-equation version was also probed numerically; it did not show an
accidental solution.

Conclusion: the collaborator's scalar volume condition is a useful diagnostic,
but the equivalence "middle maximal volume iff smooth doubling" is false as a
full matching criterion in our implementation.  Full mirror matching requires
all matching equations, not only `l(pi/6)=0`.

## Foscolo-Haskins S^6 Calibration

The project briefly switched to the Foscolo-Haskins nearly Kahler S^6 problem
to compare numerical losses against known non-homogeneous solutions.

Two approaches were implemented:

- direct terminal singular shooting;
- maximal-volume matching.

The max-volume version was more robust.  It recovered the known round and
exotic examples more cleanly and motivated adding analogous max-volume matching
for Berger and S^7.

This detour did not produce new G2 examples directly.  Its value was
methodological: it showed that matching at a stationary-volume orbit can be a
better-conditioned search target than trying to impose terminal singular
conditions directly.

## Original S^7 q_i Searches

The original S^7 work used the same `q_i` framework as Berger, with fixed
right endpoint charts for the known round and squashed homogeneous examples.

What succeeded:

- The round S^7 endpoint chart was derived and validated.
- The squashed S^7 endpoint chart was derived and validated.
- The known round and squashed solutions were recovered in fixed-chart
  calibration runs.
- Full-moduli first-jet and terminal-offset scouts were built to reduce the
  blind spots of fixed-right-chart searches.
- Max-volume versions of the S^7 scouts were also added.

What the searches showed:

- The known round and squashed examples are numerically recoverable.
- No new S^7 solution survived the scout/refinement pipelines.
- The fixed-right-chart searches are not as complete as the Berger branch
  audit, because fully parameterized S^7 right endpoint families are subtler.
  This motivated looking at other S^7 cohomogeneity-one actions instead of
  only continuing to expand the old `q_i` search.

## Podesta SU(2)^3 Action On S^7

The next major S^7 target was Podesta's `SU(2)^3` action:

```text
G = Sp(1)^3
H = diagonal Sp(1)
principal orbit = S3 x S3
singular orbits = S3 and S3
```

The compact cohomogeneity-one manifold is exactly S^7, realized as the
restriction of the standard `Sp(2) x Sp(1)` action on the unit sphere in `H^2`.

The repository records:

```text
docs/s7-su2-cubed-action-audit.md
docs/s7-su2-cubed-podesta-scout.md
docs/s7-su2-cubed-defect-audit.md
docs/s7-su2-cubed-defect-codex-response.md
docs/s7-su2-cubed-tail-defect.md
```

The Podesta invariant chart is a five-function system.  The endpoint
conditions reduce the left singular germ, after normalization, to a scalar
parameter `a`.  The known compact closures are:

```text
round S7:    a = -36
squashed S7: a = 108/5
```

The dedicated scout recovered these known closures but did not find a new
compact closure.

After the numerical search, a proof-style large-|a| exclusion attempt was
pursued.  The main defect idea was to scale the one-ended ODE and study a
terminal closure defect such as `x3(T)` or `x2(T)`.  Considerable interval and
tube machinery was built:

- moving rectangular tubes;
- restart chains;
- p-time transformations;
- carried `C` variables;
- terminal wall checks;
- Taylor start blocks;
- broad tail closure certificates.

The tail part of the argument made real progress and several conditional
segments were certified.  But the composed proof route still has a gap:
the rectangular/corridor methods lose too much correlation after about
`t ~ 2.6` in the ordinary-time bridge.  The current diagnosis is not a sign
of a new solution, but a proof-strategy obstruction.  A successful proof would
need a sharper correlated enclosure, shaped tube, new scalar identity, or a
different global argument.

This is why the current recommendation is to leave the large-|a| pen-and-paper
route alone for now.

## S^7 Action Census

After the Podesta proof route stalled, an action census was added:

```text
docs/s7-action-census.md
```

It ranks possible next cohomogeneity-one actions on S^7 by novelty,
tractability, invariant form dimension, and visibility of known round/squashed
calibrations.

The top recommendation was:

```text
Sp(1) x Sp(1) x U(1)
```

on the same `S3 x S3` orbit foliation as Podesta, because:

- it strictly weakens the already-tested Podesta symmetry;
- it still contains the Podesta five-function system as a subchart;
- it still sees both round and squashed S^7;
- its raw invariant ansatz has 13 functions, moderate compared with the
  35-function minimal `Sp(1) x Sp(1)` action.

Other candidates:

- `S(U(3) x U(1))` on `S5 x S1`: viable but less well calibrated because
  squashed visibility is unclear.
- `G2` with principal orbit S^6: tiny and good for sanity checks, but unlikely
  to produce new examples.
- minimal `Sp(1) x Sp(1)` on `S3 x S3`: interesting but probably too large
  until the intermediate U(1) case teaches us more.
- real linear `S^p x S^q` actions: discarded for lack of invariant G2-form
  room.

## Sp(1) x Sp(1) x U(1) S^7 System

The intermediate S^7 action has now been worked out far enough to scout.

Main documentation:

```text
docs/s7-sp1xsp1xu1-system.md
```

Main code:

```text
experiments/s7/sp1xsp1xu1_system.py
experiments/s7/sp1xsp1xu1_matching.py
experiments/s7/sp1xsp1xu1_scout.py
```

The principal-orbit invariant variables are:

- five invariant 2-form coefficients `x1,...,x5`;
- eight invariant 3-form coefficients `y1,...,y8`.

So the raw ansatz has 13 functions.  The algebraic nearly-parallel equations
are already restrictive.  On the regular branch with `lambda != 0` and
`x3 != 0`, they force

```text
x1 = x2 = 0.
```

After algebraic constraints, the regular branch has six algebraic degrees
before endpoint Taylor recurrences are imposed.

The Podesta subchart embeds as:

```text
x = (0, 0, f0, f0, 0)
y = (f1, f4, 0, f3, f3, f2, 0, f4)
```

Endpoint conditions were derived to leading weighted order.  The practical
matching code currently fixes five leading constants at each endpoint:

```text
A3 = X3(0)
A4 = X4(0)
B2 = Y2(0)
B4 = Y4(0)
C  = surviving-volume coefficient
```

Higher coefficients are fitted numerically against the ODE and algebraic
residuals.  This is not as clean as the exact Berger Taylor recurrence, but it
is enough for a first scout and recovers the known controls.

Known exact endpoint germs recover round and squashed S^7:

```text
round:    residual about 1e-9, interval pi/2
squashed: residual about 3e-9, interval pi/2
```

## Aloff-Wallach

Aloff-Wallach was investigated as a next topology after Berger/S^7.

Generic Aloff-Wallach spaces

```text
N_{k,l} = SU(3)/S^1_{k,l}
```

have known homogeneous nearly parallel structures, but the obvious calibrated
`SU(3)` action is transitive.  The usual proper connected subgroups preserving
the homogeneous setup are too small to give six-dimensional principal orbits.
So generic `N_{k,l}` is not immediately ready for the endpoint-volume pipeline.

The exceptional space `N_{1,1}` is more interesting.  It has a fibration

```text
SO(3) -> N_{1,1} -> CP^2
```

and the candidate action

```text
SO(3)_real x SO(3)_fiber
```

has six-dimensional generic orbits, hence cohomogeneity one.

What has been done:

- feasibility audit;
- invariant ansatz work for `N_{1,1}`;
- checks of known tri-Sasakian/squashed structures in Ball-Oliveira
  conventions;
- principal-orbit SU(3)-structure variable declaration;
- first endpoint smoothness restrictions;
- an experimental homogeneous recovery layer;
- an initial endpoint-reduced max-volume scout plumbing layer.

Important caveat: the `N_{1,1}` system is not the old `q_i` system.  It needs
its own invariant basis, ODE, endpoint charts, and calibration.  The existing
code is useful plumbing, not yet a Berger-level search with strong negative
evidence.

## Stiefel

The standard Stiefel manifold

```text
V_{5,2} = SO(5)/SO(3)
```

was checked as a possible next topology.

It has known homogeneous nearly parallel G2 structures, and the algebraic
homogeneous calibration data can be verified.  But the natural `SO(4)` action
on `V_{5,2}` has cohomogeneity two, not one.  The standard connected candidates
do not give the required six-dimensional principal orbits:

```text
SO(5): transitive
SO(4): cohomogeneity 2
U(2): too small
SO(3): too small
```

So standard Stiefel is not currently a valid endpoint-volume target for this
repository's cohomogeneity-one pipeline.  It should not be treated as a simple
"next Berger-like scout" unless a different cohomogeneity-one action is found.

## What Has Not Worked

The project has tried several broad strategies that did not lead to a new
example:

- Direct Berger midpoint shooting beyond the known solution.
- Berger mirrored and non-mirrored grids across all implemented branch regimes.
- Max-volume Berger scouting.
- Fixed-chart and moduli-expanded S^7 `q_i` scouting.
- Podesta `SU(2)^3` compact closure search beyond round/squashed.
- A Podesta large-|a| proof via scalar defects and rectangular interval tubes.
- Treating the collaborator's one-sided Berger volume condition as if it were
  sufficient for doubling.
- Treating Stiefel as if it fit the current cohomogeneity-one endpoint matcher.

None of these failures is a theorem except where an explicit dimension or
fixed-point obstruction was shown.  But the numerical evidence is now quite
substantial for the branches actually explored.

## Current Interpretation

The strongest computational conclusion is:

```text
No new nearly parallel G2 example has been found.
```

More specifically:

- Berger: strong computational evidence against non-Berger solutions in the
  implemented branch families.
- Original q_i S^7: known round/squashed recovered; no new candidate.
- Podesta SU(2)^3 S^7: known round/squashed recovered; no new compact closure
  found; proof route stalled.
- Sp(1) x Sp(1) x U(1) S^7: system and endpoint scout exist; first broad
  random scout found no new candidate.
- Aloff-Wallach `N_{1,1}`: promising but not yet searched at the same level.
- Stiefel: not currently compatible with the pipeline.

## Suggested Next Steps

The most reasonable next computational steps are:

1. For `Sp(1) x Sp(1) x U(1)`, improve endpoint germ fitting and rerun a
   smaller but more reliable scout near structured regions rather than a huge
   absolute box.
2. Add a refinement stage for the best `Sp(1) x Sp(1) x U(1)` random scouts,
   even though the current best residuals are poor, to test whether the fitter
   has hidden roughness.
3. Consider a structured grid or low-discrepancy sampler in the 10 endpoint
   constants, but only after the known controls are recovered with smaller
   residuals at the same scout settings.
4. If moving away from S^7, continue `N_{1,1}` Aloff-Wallach: firm up the ODE
   and endpoint recurrence, then recover a known homogeneous solution before
   running any broad scout.
5. Leave the Podesta large-|a| proof route alone unless a genuinely new
   correlated enclosure or scalar identity appears.

## Bottom Line

The repository has become a broad, reusable search framework for
cohomogeneity-one nearly parallel G2 structures.  It has successfully
rediscovered every known calibration example it was designed to see, but every
new-example search so far has either flowed back to a known solution, entered a
collapsed/invalid branch, or produced residuals much too large to be credible.

The best live direction is still to search other S^7 actions before returning
to more speculative topology changes.  The `Sp(1) x Sp(1) x U(1)` system is
now the most developed of those new-action attempts, but its first overnight
random scout was negative.
